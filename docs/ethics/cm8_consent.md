## 1. Purpose

This study investigates how a person's stream of reported thoughts changes over time, and
whether a brief instruction (for example, to think about something else, or a single word we
suggest) can change the direction of those thoughts. This is basic cognitive-science
research. **It is not a mind-reading test and does not predict your future.**

## 2. What you will do

- Report your thoughts in a series of short rounds, by [speaking / typing — to be confirmed],
  for about **45–60 minutes**.
- At random points, follow a brief on-screen instruction (keep thinking as usual / move your
  thinking in a different direction / think about a suggested word).
- Answer a few short questions about how the task felt.

There is no right or wrong way to think. You may skip any round or describe your thought in
general terms.

## 3. What the computer does

A program analyzes the **broad semantic direction** of the words you report and estimates
the general direction your reported thoughts are likely to continue in. It **does not read
private thoughts from your brain**, does not know what you really mean, and does not control
your mind. You are always in control of what you say or type.

## 4. Risks and discomfort

- Reporting thoughts may feel unusual or tiring; some instructions may feel mildly
  frustrating. **Minimal risk.**
- You may occasionally say or type something personal. **You are never required to share
  anything you do not wish to share**; you may skip or generalize.

## 5. Voluntary participation and withdrawal

- Participation is voluntary. You may **stop at any time** without a reason and without any
  disadvantage.
- You may **skip or withhold** individual thought reports where compatible with the task.
- If you withdraw, data collected before withdrawal are kept for analysis unless you request
  deletion within the withdrawal window (see the data statement).
