## What the study is about

People's thoughts naturally drift from one topic to another. We want to understand whether
a short instruction — for example, "think about something else," or a single word we suggest
— can change the direction your thoughts are heading. This is a basic question in cognitive
science. **This is not a mind-reading test, and it does not predict your future or your
destiny.** It studies the broad *topic direction* of your thoughts in the moments right
after an instruction.

## What will happen

- You will spend about **45–60 minutes** in a quiet room (or online, [to be confirmed]).
- You will report your thoughts in a series of short rounds, either **by speaking them out
  loud** or **by typing them** (we will tell you which before you start). There is no right
  or wrong way to think.
- At random points, you will be given a **brief instruction** on the screen. Some
  instructions will simply ask you to keep thinking as usual. Some will ask you to
  deliberately move your thinking in a different direction. Some will show you a single word
  to think about.
- A computer program will **analyze the broad semantic direction** of the words you report.
  It does **not** read your private thoughts directly from your brain, and it does not judge
  you. It only looks at the general topic of the words you choose to say or type.
- At a few points you may be asked simple questions about how hard the task felt and whether
  you felt you changed your thinking.

## What the program can and cannot do

- **Can:** describe the general topic of the thoughts you report, and estimate the broad
  direction your reported thoughts are likely to continue in.
- **Cannot:** read unspoken private thoughts, know what you really mean, predict specific
  future events, or control your mind. You are always in control of what you say or type.

## Possible discomfort

- Thinking out loud or typing your thoughts may feel a little unusual or tiring.
- Some instructions ask you to change your thinking, which may feel mildly frustrating if
  it is hard.
- You might occasionally say or type something personal. **You are never required to share
  anything you do not want to share.** You may skip any round, say "pass," or describe your
  thought in general terms.

## Taking part is voluntary

- You may **stop at any time** without giving a reason and without any disadvantage.
- You may **skip or withhold** individual thought reports where that is compatible with the
  task.
- Stopping does not affect any payment you have already earned for completed parts.

## What happens to your data

- Your reports are stored **under a study code, not your name**. The list linking codes to
  names is kept separately and locked.
- We mainly use the **meaning (semantic content)** of your reports, not the raw text, for the
  scientific analysis.
- Raw text is stored **encrypted**, access is limited to the research team, and it is kept
  only as long as needed and then deleted (or longer only with your separate agreement).
- Your results will be used in scientific publications, but **never in a way that identifies
  you**.
