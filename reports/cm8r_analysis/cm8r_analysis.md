# CM-8R17/18/21 — Sham Quality + Cue-Priming + Burden

## S17 — Sham quality
- SHAM vs CONTROL ATE: -0.0028 (sd 0.0191)
- SHAM BRP: 0.094; CONTROL BRP: 0.100
- The sham condition's ATE vs control is ~0 (the sham is a believable no-op). The sham BRP is close to the control BRP, confirming the sham does not produce a real semantic redirection. This is the desired sham quality: believable to the participant, but no real effect.

## S18 — Cue-priming defense
- CUE BRP: 0.121; CUE-CONTROL: 0.018
- The cue condition's effect is partly a LEXICAL ECHO (the cue word repeats in the post-intervention thoughts), not a real semantic branch change. The secondary diagnostics (cue-word repetition rate, divergence-after-cue-disappears) distinguish the two. The frozen BRP is the PRIMARY estimand; these diagnostics are pre-declared SECONDARY robustness analyses to defend against the cue-priming confound.

## S21 — Participant burden
- 24 trials x 4 thoughts = 96 thoughts; ~28.0 min.
- The session is ~28 minutes for 24 trials (96 thoughts). This is feasible for a human participant (a single ~30-45 min sitting). The burden is moderate; no additional burden is added by the oracle or the forecaster (both run locally and offline).
